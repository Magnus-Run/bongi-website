Wave II Series

A type-approved touchscreen display, built for the bridge.

A series of marine-grade LED displays with full glass fronts and PCAP multitouch, designed for commercial vessels, offshore platforms, and naval ships. Where display hardware must perform reliably across the full service life of the vessel.

Marine Display Type-Approved IEC 60945
View specifications → Request a data sheet →

Engineered to the standard, not adapted to it.

The Wave II is built into Wärtsilä's NACOS Platinum integrated bridge system. It won a Red Dot Design Award in 2023. The design is based on feedback from shipowners, shipyards, system integrators, and the navigators working on the bridge.

  • Full glass front with optional PCAP multitouch.
  • Individually colour-calibrated by gamma curve, out of production.
  • IEC 60945 certified and DNV type-approved.

Mounting

One patented system, three installations.

The Wave II mounting system covers the full range of bridge and wheelhouse layouts without requiring custom fabrication.

Table mount

Surface installation on a bridge console or workstation, at the navigator's working height.

Ceiling mount

Overhead installation on the hinge system for overhead display positions above the console line.

Flush console mount

Recessed installation for fully integrated bridge consoles where the glass front sits level with the panel.

Certifications

Approved for the conditions at sea.

IEC 60945

The international standard for environmental requirements on navigational equipment. It covers water ingress, vibration, temperature extremes, and electromagnetic compatibility. In plain terms: the display keeps working through the conditions a bridge actually meets at sea. All Wave II monitors are certified to it.

DNV Type Approval

Type approval from DNV, the maritime classification society, confirms the model has been independently assessed and approved for installation on classed vessels. Wave II monitors of the relevant sizes carry this approval. DNV is the body formerly known as DNV GL.
